# Launch-year source audit

This packet maps every row in `data/studies/13-errata-dispositions/raw/families.csv` to the introduction year of the named reference product family associated with its specification or revision guide. It retains 19 source artifacts, including official Intel and AMD press releases and press kits, plus a manifest recording the canonical URL, final retrieval URL, HTTP status, content type, byte count, retrieval date, and SHA-256 for each artifact. HTML pages are retained as HTML; four Intel archive items are retained in their original PDF format.

`launch_years.csv` includes one row per input family, the source URL and local file, source hash, an exact source excerpt under 25 words, and a scope note. Launch dates denote the official launch or family-introduction date where the source states one; the year is the plot variable. Some announcements precede retail availability, as noted in the row. Broadwell-EP is dated to Intel’s March 31, 2016 E5-2600 v4 product announcement; the exact day is an announcement date, not an independently verified first retail sale. Skylake-SP has year-only support in the retained Intel developer source, so `launch_date` is blank rather than inferred from secondary chronologies.

The family mapping follows the input document titles and Intel/AMD generation names. Coffee Lake combines 8th- and 9th-generation desktop Core updates, so 2017 is the earliest covered Coffee Lake product availability; Raptor Lake combines 13th and 14th generation updates, so 2022 is the earliest family launch and 2023 products are a later extension. Ice Lake client and Ice Lake-SP remain separate. Tiger Lake and Rocket Lake remain separate despite both being 11th generation because their specifications cover mobile and desktop product families, respectively.

AMD revision guides identify processor-family/model ranges, while the plot labels represent server product families. The Zen 1 guide’s Family 17h scope can include products beyond server EPYC, including Threadripper; the row note preserves this scope distinction. The launch year locates the named architecture generation, not every product covered by a specification document.

No family was excluded. The row-level source excerpts and hashes make the assignments independently checkable. The source packet is descriptive evidence for launch chronology; it does not support a causal interpretation of errata counts by age.
